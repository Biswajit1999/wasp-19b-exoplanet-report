01 · Principal result

The simpler ephemeris remains preferred.

The seven-sector dataset independently times 204 transits across 2,528 days. A quadratic ephemeris gives a conditional period derivative of −1.38 ± 4.02 ms yr⁻¹, but its additional curvature parameter is not justified by the likelihood gain: ΔBIC(linear − quadratic) = −5.20.

Under the stated Gaussian and equilibrium-tide assumptions, the 95% negative bound is Ṗ > −9.26 ms yr⁻¹, corresponding to Q′★ > 3.0 × 105. This is a sensitivity limit—not a tidal-decay detection.

02 · Robustness

No single sector determines the timing conclusion.

Seven leave-one-sector-out refits probe sensitivity to individual observing campaigns. Every deletion prefers the linear model: ΔBIC ranges from −4.84 to −5.17, while Ṗ ranges from −2.66 to −0.17 ms yr⁻¹. This test addresses sector leverage; it does not capture every shared systematic.

03 · Measurement chain

From archive light curves to an auditable timing bound.

01

Homogeneous input

Seven target-specific, 120-second SPOC light curves are kept unmodified. Mixed-cadence and FFI products are excluded to avoid duplicate or heterogeneous measurements.

02

Sector-level transit

Each sector receives a bounded circular, quadratic-limb-darkened fit with timing freedom and a local baseline. Residual time-averaging beta inflates formal errors.

03

Event timing

The sector shape is fixed while midpoint, intercept, and slope are fitted for every transit. Empirical sector jitter prevents a few formally precise events from dominating.

04

Ephemeris comparison

Conditioned weighted least squares compares linear and quadratic O−C models. BIC penalizes the quadratic model's extra coefficient.

05

Deletion analysis

Both ephemerides are refitted seven times, omitting one sector per run. The full inference is retained alongside the deletion ensemble.

06

Fail-closed checks

SHA-256 tests verify every FITS file. Regression tests rerun the real-data analysis and require all declared figures and tables.

04 · Literature context

The atmospheric evidence is not one settled detection.

WASP-19 b illustrates why cross-instrument reproducibility and stellar activity matter. The studies below use different instruments, epochs, wavelength ranges, reduction choices, and model spaces. Broadband TESS photometry cannot adjudicate molecular abundances, so this repository does not turn those claims into a new retrieval.

06 · Scope

What this analysis does not establish.

  • The orbit is circular and quadratic limb-darkening coefficients are fixed representative values.
  • Composite semi-major axis and stellar-radius uncertainties are not propagated.
  • Midpoint freedom introduces a bounded timing search; BIC is used as the support gate.
  • PDCSAP processing, dilution and stellar variability can bias inferred geometry.
  • Sector-level detrending creates shared systematics among event timings.
  • Empirical jitter reduces formal overconfidence but does not make timings independent.
  • The Ṗ interval is conditional on a quadratic ephemeris and diagonal timing errors.
  • The analysis cannot reject apsidal precession or replace longer heterogeneous timing baselines.
